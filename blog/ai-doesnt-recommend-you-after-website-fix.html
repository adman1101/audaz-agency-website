You Fixed Your Website. AI Still Doesn't Recommend You. Here's Why.

By Rene Galan, Co-Founder & AI Consulting Director Published September 16, 2026 Updated September 27, 2026
Fixing your website teaches AI how to describe you. It doesn't teach AI to trust you. Trust comes from what everyone else says about your business — reviews, directories, and community platforms — and AI engines weight those sources far more heavily than your own homepage. Most small businesses have never touched this half of AI visibility.

We fixed the site. The website still wasn't enough.

Last issue, we rewrote our own homepage line by line, added FAQ content built around the questions people actually ask, and shipped schema markup across every page. It worked exactly the way it was supposed to: ask ChatGPT to describe AUDAZ today and it gets it right — bilingual AI consulting, South Florida, GEO/AEO.

Then we asked the harder question: "who should I hire for AI visibility in South Florida?" We ran it on ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity. Nineteen agencies came back across the three engines. Zero overlap between them. AUDAZ wasn't on any of the three lists.

ChatGPT told us flatly why, when we asked what it weighs before recommending an agency instead of just describing one: "entity clarity, authoritative third-party mentions, reviews, structured data, local signals, useful content, and how the business actually shows up when I'm asked about it." Five of those seven live somewhere other than the business's own website.

Why doesn't a correct description turn into a recommendation?

Describing a business is a lookup. The website is a reasonably trustworthy source for what a business says about itself, and AI is good at reading it back accurately. Recommending a business is a judgment call, and a judgment call needs corroboration — something confirming from outside the business that it's real, active, and worth naming. A great résumé with no references gets a polite description and no callback.

Where does AI actually get its trust signals?

A 2026 Peec AI analysis of roughly 30 million citations across the major AI engines found the same pattern we found in our own testing: the most heavily cited sources were Reddit, YouTube, and LinkedIn — community and third-party platforms where people talk about businesses, not where businesses talk about themselves.

When Perplexity described AUDAZ, it didn't cite audaz.agency. It cited our Clutch profile — a directory listing with exactly one verified client review.

What does this mean for a business with no Reddit presence?

You don't need to be famous on Reddit. You need the corroboration signals AI is actually checking for, and most small businesses are missing all of them at once:

  • Conflicting information across platforms — a different address or phone number on Google than on Facebook
  • Reviews sitting on one platform only, instead of spread across two or three
  • A LinkedIn company page that hasn't been touched in a year
  • Zero independent, external writing that describes what the business actually does

How do you start building off-site trust without it feeling like spam?

  • Make every listing agree. Same name, address, and phone number on Google, Yelp, BBB, LinkedIn, and every industry directory you're in. AI cross-checks this before it trusts any of it.
  • Put your reviews in more than one place. Reviews concentrated entirely on Google read as thinner than the same number spread across Google, Facebook, and an industry-specific directory.
  • Show up where your customers actually talk. Genuine participation in the communities your customers use, before you ever mention what you sell.
  • Get someone else to write about you. An independent article describing what you do outweighs anything you publish about yourself.

If you do only one thing from this issue: make your name, address, and phone number match everywhere you're listed, then get one independent review or article that isn't on your own site.

Frequently asked questions

Is this different from backlinks and SEO?

Related but not the same. Traditional SEO valued backlinks as a vote of authority. AI engines care about off-site mentions as corroboration — confirming what your website already claims through sources you don't control. A link helps. A sentence describing what you do, written by someone else, helps more.

Do I need to be on Reddit, YouTube, and LinkedIn specifically?

No. You need to be wherever your customers and peers actually talk. A contractor gets more from an active Google Business Profile and a neighborhood Facebook group than from a Reddit account nobody reads.

How long before this changes what AI says about me?

Website fixes show up in days. Off-site trust usually takes one to three months of consistent presence before an AI engine treats you as an established answer rather than an unverified claim.

Does this apply to AI search in Spanish too?

Yes, and the opportunity is bigger. Fewer businesses have built any Spanish-language off-site presence, so even a modest bilingual footprint stands out.

Can I fix this myself?

The listing cleanup takes an afternoon. Knowing what AI currently says about you and where it's getting that information is the part worth having someone else check first.
