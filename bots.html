HOW WE BUILD THEM

What makes a bot worth building

Most business chatbots fail for the same reason: they don't know anything specific. They deflect, they loop, and customers give up and call anyway. The difference is what the bot is actually connected to.

Trained on your real operation

Every property, service, policy, and quirk — including the small stuff staff answer a hundred times a week. If your team knows it, the bot knows it.

Connected to live data

Booking systems, property management platforms, availability. The bot answers from what's true right now, not a document written six months ago.

Memory across conversations

It remembers who it's talking to and what was already discussed, so returning customers don't start from zero every time.

Guardrails that hold

Payment info, door codes, WiFi passwords, IDs, health data — filtered and blocked before they ever reach a response. Non-negotiable.

CASE — VACATION RENTAL CONCIERGE

A 26-property concierge bot in the Florida Keys

A vacation rental management company was fielding the same guest questions around the clock — check-in times, WiFi, appliance quirks, local recommendations — across dozens of individual properties, each with its own details.

We built a bilingual concierge bot trained on every unit, integrated with their property management platform, with a memory layer so returning guests aren't treated like strangers.

Every property, individually

Trained unit by unit with property-specific detail — not generic answers dressed up as personalization.

Live system integration

Connected directly to their booking and property management platform, so answers reflect current reality.

Memory layer

Guest context carries across conversations, so repeat guests pick up where they left off.

Redaction filter

Payment details, door codes, WiFi passwords, government IDs, and health data blocked at the source.

Embedded on their own site

Dropped into their existing website — no platform migration, no rebuild required.

Bilingual from day one

English and Spanish built in from the start — not a translation layer added later.
